Our Research

The Stromal-Immunoengineering Lab (SIEL) investigates immune cell and fibroblast crosstalk in fibrosis and tissue repair. We use high-dimensional approaches and biomaterial platforms to study these interactions during fibrosis and regeneration.

Our areas of interest include pulmonary fibrosis, biomaterial-mediated fibrosis, and endometriosis. We aim to identify cell populations and signaling pathways that can inform new diagnostic and therapeutic approaches, and to engineer biomaterial-based human models of fibrotic disorders.

Matrix Biology Plus · 2025

Crosstalk Between T Cells and Fibroblasts in Biomaterial-Mediated Fibrosis

Kibet, Mathew & Daniel Abebayehu · Article 100172

Key takeaway

T cells and fibroblasts engage in bidirectional signaling that shapes biomaterial-driven fibrotic encapsulation. Targeting their crosstalk may help reduce fibrosis and improve implant performance.

Why it matters

Fibrotic encapsulation can isolate implants such as stents, sensors, and scaffolds. T cell–fibroblast interactions are an important, underappreciated part of this process.

Main components

  • T cell subsets: CD4+, CD8+, regulatory, and tissue-resident types can differentially affect fibroblast activity.
  • Fibroblast populations: activated myofibroblasts and matrix-producing subsets respond to immune cues.
  • Signals: cytokines, cell contact, and matrix remodeling create feedback loops.

Mechanisms highlighted

  • T cell cytokine signaling can modulate fibroblast activation.
  • Fibroblasts can recruit and shape T cell responses.
  • Extracellular matrix stiffness and composition affect immune cell behavior.

Models & technologies

Emerging tools to probe crosstalk

  • Co-culture systems and organoids
  • Single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics
  • Tunable biomaterials
